### GCHQ Confrontation (2012–2013)

In late 2012, the National Technical Assistance Centre (NTAC) — a division of GCHQ and liaison with the Home Office — demanded the encryption keys for Spector's **PrivateSky** secure email service under a **RIPA warrant signed by the Home Secretary**.

**Response:** Rather than build a backdoor, Spector shut down PrivateSky entirely — months before Lavabit and Silent Circle faced similar choices, and before the Snowden revelations.

**Technical Solution:** Spector co-developed **M-Pin split-key technology** — the root key is split so the company holds one half and the user holds the other. If intelligence services demand key surrender, the company can comply but the key is useless without the user's half.

**Relevance to Current Operation:** Spector publicly defied UK intelligence services and built technology specifically designed to resist what he believed was surveillance state overreach. Nothing he did during this time period was unlawful. According to a LinkedIn profile (one of two) visible in the UK, Chris Cook received his OBE in February 2024 for work on pushing the Investigatory Powers Act ("Snooper's Charter") through Parliament. Spector productized technology that defeats exactly what the Snooper's Charter enables and was vocally against the passage of the bill during consultation periods. This may be a factor in his selection as a target.
