## The Argentine IP Law Trap

### Legal Background

Argentine copyright law (Ley 11.723) contains *derecho moral* (moral rights) provisions that cannot be contractually waived. Under this framework:

| U.S. Work-for-Hire | Argentine Moral Rights |
|--------------------|------------------------|
| Employer owns all IP created during employment | Designer retains inalienable moral rights regardless of contract |
| Written agreement transfers all rights | Written agreement cannot extinguish moral rights |
| Clean chain of title | Designer can claim co-ownership, profit participation, or attribution |
| Standard contractor agreement sufficient | Even explicit IP assignment leaves designer with enforceable claims |

### The Exploitation Pattern

1. **Target companies hiring Argentine contractors** for brand/visual work

2. **Contractor creates assets** under work-for-hire agreement

3. **Contractor later claims moral rights** under Argentine law

4. **Assets are "legitimately" transferred** to the beneficiary operation

5. **Original company cannot assert clean title** because Argentine designer has standing to claim co-ownership

**This is not a loophole—it's a deliberate mechanism for "legal" IP theft.**
